# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Mixed general public arriving in Egypt across five governorates (Cairo, Alexandria, Minya, Assiut, Tanta): prospective members (graduates, business leaders/managers, teachers, lawyers, doctors) evaluating whether a leadership group fits them, plus donors/partners checking legitimacy and impact, plus general/curious visitors and press. Bilingual audience — Arabic-first for most local visitors, English for wider reach.

## Product Purpose

Leader Strategies is the web presence for a Christian organization in Egypt focused on spiritual service and leadership development across professional sectors (Graduates, Business Leaders & Managers, Teachers, Lawyers, Doctors) and city boards. The site must communicate legitimacy, mission, and real human impact clearly enough that a first-time visitor understands what the organization does, trusts it, and knows how to get involved (join a sector group, read about a city board, contact/partner). Success = visitors leaving with a clear, credible, emotionally resonant picture of the mission — not a generic templated NGO site.

## Brand Personality

Warm, human, hopeful. Voice is sincere and dignified, not corporate or salesy, not overproduced. Real stories and real people over stock abstraction. Should feel like a living, growing community rooted in faith and service — approachable, not preachy or heavy-handed.

## Anti-references

Must not look like a generic AI-generated SaaS template: no purple/blue gradient hero blobs, no icons-in-circles feature grids, no hero-metric-card clichés, no stock "team high-fiving" photography, no gradient text, no glassmorphism-by-default. Also avoid the opposite failure mode of a flat, dated, boxy government/NGO site with no visual personality. And avoid an overproduced corporate-megachurch feel (autoplay video hero, glossy oversized CTAs).

## Design Principles

- Warmth over polish-for-its-own-sake: real photography and real names/stories carry more trust than abstract iconography.
- One system, two directions: Arabic (RTL, Rubik) and English (LTR) must feel like the same brand executed natively in each direction, not a mirrored afterthought.
- Legitimacy through clarity: mission, sectors, and governance (city boards) should be scannable and concrete, not vague mission-statement fog.
- Restrained color, confident typography: let a distinctive type/color system do the "not AI-generated" work rather than decorative effects.
- Every page earns a next step: visitors should always have an obvious, low-pressure way to learn more or connect, never a dead end.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: solid color contrast in both light Arabic and English type, readable Rubik sizing/line-height (not just a font-swap of the Latin scale), visible keyboard focus states, no accessibility barriers assumed beyond a general mixed-age public audience.
